The Deck That
Follows You

A normal HTML deck on a projector. Scan the code and it is also on your phone, on the slide the presenter is on right now — and their phone is the remote.

To go live
Press L on the deck, then point a camera at the code that appears.
Stage to phone
About 80 milliseconds.
How it works

Three Screens, Three Jobs

  • StageThe laptop on the projector. It owns the current slide and hands out both links as codes on screen.
  • RemoteThe presenter's own phone. Next and back, their notes, a timer and a pointer — and nobody else can drive.
  • AudienceEveryone else's phones. The same slide, close up and readable, without squinting at the wall.

Nothing to install. Every role is the same web page, opened at a different link.

The one step

Scan Once, Follow Everything

A four-character room code and a camera. From then on, every time the presenter moves, your screen moves.

  1. They press LTwo codes appear on the projector — one for the room, one private.
  2. You scan the left onePoint a camera at it and tap the link that pops up.
  3. That is itNo app, no sign-in, no typing out a long address.

The presenter's panel shows how many phones are in the room, live.

In my hand

My Phone Is the Clicker

No more standing next to the laptop. Everything a presenter view gives you, on the device already in your hand.

  • My notes, in full sizeThe same notes already written into the slide — you write them once.
  • What is coming nextThe next slide's title, so a transition never catches you out.
  • A running clockStarted the moment the deck went live, not when you opened the file.
  • Drag to pointA dot lands on the projected slide, exactly where you aimed.

Only the phone that scanned the private code can move the deck. A room code on its own does nothing.

Your call

How Much They Get to Wander

  • LockedThey watch, only. Swiping does nothing. For when the order of the argument matters.
  • Follow — the defaultThey follow, but may stray back a slide to re-read, then tap one button to catch up.
  • OpenThe whole deck is theirs. For a workshop, or the moment you stop talking and they start reading.

Switch between them mid-sentence. There is also a quiet "I'm lost" tap that only the presenter sees.

The boring part that matters

Nothing Loads Mid-Sentence

Before the deck will call itself live it goes through every slide behind a cover: fonts resolved, images decoded, each slide actually drawn once, the screen locked awake, the connection joined.

And if the wifi gives out, the sync quietly stops and the deck keeps working like any other deck. It can never take the presentation down with it.
